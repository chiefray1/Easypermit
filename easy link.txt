2. Business objective
The website must function as a digital customer acquisition and conversion platform.

It must:
1. Attract prospective international students and travellers.
2. Help visitors identify the right Easy Permit service.
3. Collect and qualify enquiries automatically.
4. Move prospects into consultations, quotations and payments.
5. Enable the team to track customers from first contact to completed service.
The long-term commercial ambition is to support 100 clients monthly and more than ₦50 million in average monthly revenue.

These are growth targets, not guaranteed outcomes.

6. Lead generation and customer journeys
Students and travellers have different buying motivations. They must not be forced through the same enquiry process.
Journey A: Study Abroad
1. Visitor discovers Easy Permit through Google or social media.
2. Visits Study Abroad.
3. Completes academic profile assessment.
4. Receives acknowledgement and consultation invitation.
5. Education adviser reviews academic and financial profile.
6. Student receives recommended options and service proposal.
7. Student accepts and makes agreed payment.
8. Application processing and progress updates begin.
9. Admission, visa guidance and pre-departure support.
Target outcome: Paying student client.

Student assessment fields
- Full name.
- Phone and email.
- Country of residence.
- Highest qualification.
- Graduation year.
- Course of interest.
- Preferred destination.
- Preferred intake.
- Available study budget.
- English language qualification, if applicable.
- Additional information.
Do not automatically reject students based on budget or lack of an English test result.

These are qualification factors for an adviser.
Journey B: Visa Customer
1. Visitor lands on a country-specific visa page.
2. Selects visa type and proposed travel dates.
3. Completes visa assessment.
4. Lead enters visa pipeline.
5. Consultant reviews applicant circumstances.
6. Client receives service scope, fees and checklist.
7. Client confirms and pays.
8. Documentation and application preparation begin.
9. Client receives progress updates and final outcome follow-up.
Target outcome: Paying visa consultancy client.
Journey C: Flight Customer
1. Selects flight enquiry.
2. Provides departure and destination information.
3. Receives acknowledgement.
4. Adviser prepares available options.
5. Client receives quotation.
6. Client confirms preferred flight.
7. Payment and ticket issuance.
8. Booking confirmation and after-sales support.
Journey D: Holiday Customer
1. Views destination or package.
2. Selects holiday type and travel period.
3. Completes group size and budget details.
4. Receives personalised proposal.
5. Confirms booking.
6. Receives itinerary, payment information and travel documentation.

Zoho integration implementation
Preferred architecture: Supabase Edge Functions connected to the Zoho CRM API.

Workflow:
1. Website submits assessment.
2. Supabase validates data.
3. Server-side function sends lead to Zoho.
4. Zoho returns lead record identifier.
5. Supabase stores submission reference.
6. User receives successful submission message.
7. Integration failures enter retry queue.
8. Administrator receives failure notification.
Use Zoho OAuth securely.

All access tokens and secrets must remain server-side.

If credentials are unavailable, implement the integration structure and document the missing configuration. Never simulate a successful CRM submission.

Security requirements
- Enable Row Level Security on sensitive tables.
- Public users can submit enquiries but cannot access other customers' information.
- Staff access requires authentication.
- Only authorised administrators can edit website content.
- Keep integration secrets in server-side environment variables.
- Rate-limit forms.
- Implement spam protection.
- Do not expose customer information in analytics events.
- Never store sensitive application documents in public storage.
Do not build full client document management in the first release. That should be a separate authenticated feature in a later phase.
